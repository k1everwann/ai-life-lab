What can you do after one lesson or two?

Two consecutive 60-minute lessons, with no programming knowledge required. Phone-based everyday tasks and webpage building are separate options.

  • One lesson: read one dataset, classify and total it, add your own follow-up, and save a source-backed analysis.
  • Two lessons: compare periods, separate observed changes from possible causes, and apply the method to travel or market data.

Connectors bring in data; analysis still needs source checks

  1. Prepare a teaching source. Download the fictional CSV below. An instructor may place it in a teaching cloud drive or document. Test with one file first.
  2. Confirm the connector and account in your actual AI tool. Use the intended teaching account and its available permissions. Learners do not need to connect private card or brokerage accounts in class.
  3. Specify the file and scope. Supply an accessible teaching-file link or name and period. First ask AI to list the fields, source rows, and missing data it actually read. Finding a filename alone does not confirm that its contents were read.
  4. Short question → follow-up → verification. Ask one question below. Read the draft, add your own requirement, then check currency, period, per-person versus whole-group costs, shares versus contracts, refunds, and exclusions.
  5. Save results and evidence. Record the source file, row IDs, formulas, known results, and unknowns. Reopen the saved record.

No connector available? Give the same downloaded CSV to an available AI tool, or use “Copy data + short request” to paste it into a conversation. Without AI, calculate and check the prepared answer first; record the AI step as not yet completed.

Available features depend on your tool, account, and permissions. Instructors demonstrate reading specified teaching data, confirm the correct file, then compare. Retrieving data, generating an AI draft, and checking fixed calculations are separate steps.

Read the data dictionary and calculation rules (English). CSV field names and values are shared English source codes in both language versions.
