Preparation 01

Course goal and proposal

A short talk on everyday AI for beginners. The phone and computer routes each take 60 minutes, including a shared opening, practice, and checking.

Help each learner complete one small, useful task and personally check whether the result is reliable and usable.

A thirty-second explanation for management

Show what AI can do through everyday examples, then guide one small exercise. Learners can reuse the materials after class with a topic of their own.

RouteDevice and preparationTakeaway
A Phone: everyday tasksA phone and an available text AI; no connector needed for the core exerciseA personally checked task draft and a request reusable next time
B Computer: build a webpageA computer with Codex, Claude Code, or another coding AI ready to useAn HTML file, one revision, and a hands-on test record

Teaching and sharing entry point

https://k1everwann.github.io/ai-life-lab/en/

Open the main page, show the projects, then choose A or B. This guide provides schedules, fallbacks, and downloads. The routes are alternatives for different sessions; one instructor is not expected to teach both simultaneously.

Three things to check first

• Confirm learner devices and tools work. Finish installation and login before class.

• Demonstrate connectors using a teaching account and fictional data only. Never project private email.

• Ask people with different experience levels to try the flow. Record where they get stuck and how long it takes. Teaching effectiveness has not yet been validated.

Preparation 02

Shared opening and project presentation notes

Minutes 0–3 focus only on investment research and the study website, about 90 seconds each. Explain principles in minutes 3–7. For each example, explain the original problem, usable result, roles of AI and tools, and one small step learners can start today.

Three shared principles

• A model learns patterns from examples and generates answers or code from current instructions and material. Fluency does not guarantee correctness.

• Connectors and tools determine accessible data and available actions. Do not assume unauthorized data has been read.

• People must return to originals, calculate totals, and operate the result. Listing a task does not complete it; generating code does not prove it works.

In the investment research illustration, an AI timeout rejects the entry candidate; risk exits do not wait for AI. An unknown previous status blocks new entries. Show no prices, performance, or trading advice. Start today with a checking list or small quiz.

All demonstration material is a labeled fictional reconstruction, not a production-system screenshot. Do not open private server dashboards, wardrobes, real emails, or deployment settings during class. This example is not investment advice.

Four learner checks

• Get a draft using a short request, then personally add a requirement. In adult-operated groups, the child states the request and the adult sends it. Record platform operation separately.

• Return to a notice, distinguish a registration deadline from an activity date, and fix an error or omission.

• Identify at least one fact that cannot yet be determined. Do not accept a time invented by AI.

• Explain why the publisher also needs checking. Save the corrected list and find it again. Search is not guaranteed complete; a list does not mean tasks were performed.

When learners fall behind

Five steps: copy → switch to AI, press and hold to paste, then send → return to the lesson → compare notices and request a revision → paste the result into the takeaway card. Preserve judgment practice for adult-operated and no-account groups. Do not count it as personal platform operation.

Keep Gmail and Drive in the formal lesson at five minutes each. Rehearse login and app switching first. Use the same notices without adding another scenario. At the end, copy into notes or download, then reopen to confirm the result was saved.

Preparation 04

A Practice data and checking answers

All data is fictional. The scenario date is 2026-11-02; dates and times use Asia/Taipei.

NoticeContent
A / Nov 2 / Community nature observationActivity Nov 8 (Sun), 09:00–12:00. Registration closes Nov 5 (Thu), 18:00. Use the community form; success requires a confirmation email.
B / Nov 1 / Old craft-class noticeOriginally Nov 7 (Sat), 10:00, first-floor classroom.
C / Nov 2 / Craft-class correctionUpdates B to Nov 7 (Sat), 14:00, second-floor classroom. Reply by Nov 4 (Wed), 20:00, about attendance.
D / Nov 2 / Pickup reminderCollect at the designated store before Nov 6 (Fri), as stated. Opening hours and the exact final pickup time are not provided.

Start short, then add conditions

First ask: “Please organize notices A–D into a task list.” Return to the originals to check deadlines, versions, and unknown times. Then write one follow-up requirement, such as: “Include the notice source and mark unspecified times as to confirm.”

A combined task to consult after revising

Use only notices A–D to draft tasks. Include what to do, the action deadline, activity time, source, and information to confirm. Corrections take priority over old notices. Separate activity dates from registration deadlines. Mark unspecified times or statuses as “to confirm”; do not invent them. Organize only: do not register, send replies, or pay.

Reference answers and common mistakes

• Activity registration: deadline Nov 5, 18:00; activity Nov 8, 09:00–12:00; source A. Success still requires a confirmation email.

• Craft-class reply: deadline Nov 4, 20:00; class Nov 7, 14:00, second floor. Source C updates B. Whether a reply has already been sent is unknown.

• Parcel: collect before Nov 6 as stated, source D. Opening hours and the exact final pickup time are unknown.

The checking answers are, in order: contradicted, supported, cannot determine yet. Matching the original establishes fidelity only. If the original is a forwarded group message, confirm dates and updates with the organizer; a forward is not an official notice.

Preparation 05

A Connectors and cloud-data demonstrations

Gmail: about five minutes

Use teaching emails prepared in advance. Check the logged-in account and allowed actions, specify the time range, then demonstrate opening one original email to verify a task.

Find emails accessible in this search from the last seven days. List matters needing my reply or action, with original email sources, deadlines, and search scope. Read does not mean completed. Mark unclear deadlines “to confirm.” Read and organize only.

Do not claim the entire inbox was scanned or require the class to authorize private accounts. If the connector fails, demonstrate using the same fictional notice pack; do not spend ten minutes troubleshooting authorization.

Google Drive: about five minutes

Put budget data in a teaching Drive in advance and identify documents by link. Finding a filename does not mean reading its contents. Confirm dates, headcount, currency, and pricing units before analysis.

Before class

• Rehearse on actual iPhone / Android devices, planned accounts, and the network. Availability depends on plan, region, plugins, and permissions.

• Adults perform ChatGPT interactions for 12-year-olds; children state requirements and check results. Explain this before the entry link. Without an adult, use the prepared draft. Do not ask learners to borrow accounts.

• Read designated teaching data only. Sending mail, registering, paying, and creating calendar events are outside this lesson.

Preparation

• Confirm Codex, Claude Code, or another tool can log in, receive requests, and supply code. VS Code is an editor; separately confirm available AI capability.

• Prepare a new practice folder and browser. Do not spend class installing tools, handling API keys, or configuring cloud environments.

• Local and cloud Codex environments access different files. Check where the actual tool will save the project.

Explain four places before the first attempt

A Build request is instruction text. The AI conversation or coding tool receives it. An HTML file stores the generated code. A browser opens the file and lets the learner operate it. Use the six-step beginner guide to demonstrate the complete journey.

When copying text output, exclude Markdown fences such as ```html and ```. Save UTF-8 plain text as index.html, not index.html.txt. Windows Notepad: Save As, file type “All files,” and show filename extensions to check. macOS TextEdit: “Make Plain Text” before saving. These are general file-saving instructions; rehearse with the actual classroom devices.

Smallest project

One index.html containing a title, purpose, and at least three items. It supports checking, unchecking, live counting, and Reset all, and is readable on a narrow screen. The first version does not keep checked progress after refreshing or closing and must clearly say so. A saved HTML file, kept checkbox progress, and a published website are three separate states.

Finished example: examples/my-checklist.html. Starter: examples/starter.html. The starter deliberately contains content only so learners can ask AI to add interactions.

Preparation 07

B Build request, tests, and fallback plans

In a new practice folder, create a single index.html for my travel preparation checklist. Include a purpose and at least three items. Allow checking and unchecking, update the completed count immediately, and provide Reset all. Use English and support a 390px phone width. Keep HTML, CSS, and JavaScript in one file, with no external packages, APIs, login, or web fonts. The first version does not keep checked progress after refreshing or closing; explain this on screen. Work only in the practice folder and do not deploy. List the tests I should perform myself after completion.

Four observable checks

CheckHands-on actionSuccess condition
ReopenClose and reopen your HTML fileYour title and at least three items appear
Check and uncheckCheck two items, then uncheck oneThe count changes to 2, then 1
Reset allCheck at least one item, then choose Reset allAll checks clear and the count is 0
Narrow screen and revisionNarrow the window; make and retest one custom revisionContent is readable, buttons work, and affected features are correct

How to describe a problem

What I did → What I saw → What I expected. Only if it actually happens: “After checking two items, the display still says 0. I expected 2. Please fix counting and keep my title and list.” If counting already works, use a real feature request instead: “Add Bring a small towel, keep existing items and interactions, and explain how to check the new count and reset.”

Fallback plans

• Cannot open the file: check that the filename is index.html rather than index.html.txt and save as plain text.

• The tool cannot edit files directly: ask for complete HTML, copy it into an editor, and save the file.

• AI is unavailable: compare starter and finished example; practice requests and tests. Record that the AI-building step is incomplete.

• preview differs from the standalone file: the isolated frame limits some features. Use trusted practice code only and test storage after downloading.

Public sharing is an after-class extension. Remove private content before choosing a publishing method. Generating, saving, preview, and publication are different stages.

Preparation 08

Classroom preparation and sources

Before and during class

• Try the main flow on a computer, phone, and projector. Confirm public URLs, PDFs, examples, and copy buttons work.

• Choose one main route after the shared seven minutes. For mixed devices, provide the other route for self-study or an assistant; one instructor should not present both simultaneously.

• Offer prompts according to experience, without judging ability by age. Rotate operator and checker roles.

• Free text and HTML are not saved automatically. The takeaway card includes today's result. Copy to notes or download ai-life-takeaway.txt, then have learners reopen it to confirm.

Material and boundaries

The course direction draws on personal AI workshop notes about Vibe Coding and Google Workspace, checked against project READMEs and maintenance documentation. The public version contains feature summaries only, without private notes, accounts, host information, or source data.

Demonstrations are fictional reconstructions without private screenshots or embeds. Investment research follows the public community version, not a private production system. N1 or collection counts are not evidence of learning effectiveness.

After-class practice

Help beginners continue after the lesson

Before asking for independent work, confirm that learners can distinguish four places: Build request text, the AI conversation or coding tool, the saved HTML file, and the browser showing the webpage. Use the beginner guide to walk through getting complete HTML, saving UTF-8 index.html, and reopening it.

Ask each learner to check two items, uncheck one, then Reset all. The visible count must change 2 → 1 → 0. Saving the HTML file does not automatically keep checked progress. Ask learners to close and reopen the file before making any persistence claim.

Report a bug only after actually observing it. If the sample count already works, request a real feature, such as adding a checklist item, and test the affected behavior. Record whether learners understood the explanation, used a prepared fallback, or personally completed the action; these are different outcomes.

Everyday data analysis: two consecutive 60-minute lessons

Three fictional cases cover card spending, travel plans, and market trading volumes. Lesson 1 uses September only to identify consumption, refunds, payments, categories, and source rows. Lesson 2 adds October to compare known amounts and missing data, then transfers the method to either travel or market data.

See the everyday-analysis lesson plans for schedules, completion criteria, a data dictionary, short requests and follow-ups, prepared answers, and calculations. Confirm the teaching account and connector read scope in the actual AI tool; downloading or pasting the same data is an equivalent fallback.
