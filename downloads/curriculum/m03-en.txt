Which group message can you trust?

When a group mixes forwarded messages and suggestions, find what you can actually act on.

What you will make

Make a source-backed school game schedule separating confirmed facts from questions.

Example result

Check-in: 11/21 09:00, the club room.
Team name: submit by 11/19 19:00; ask about the game start.

This is a prepared example. Your AI may use different wording.

Practice material

Fictional data; time zone Asia/Taipei.
D | issued 10/20: check in at the club room on 2026/11/21 at 10:00.
B | organizer approved 10/25: replaces D; check in at the club room on 2026/11/21 at 09:00; submit a team name by 11/19 19:00; game format undecided.
E | discussion draft 10/28: suggests check-in on 11/21 at 08:30; not approved.
F | game-start note: starts 11/21 at 09:30; approval status not stated.

Request for AI

Turn these four messages into a table: action / time / note. Put organizer-confirmed arrangements first, then suggestions and questions.

1. Paste the four messages and request into AI to get a table.
2. Look back at organizer message B: the check-in is 09:00; E’s 08:30 is a suggestion. Check that the table keeps them separate.
3. Choose one unconfirmed detail, write a question that would resolve it, and add a source ID to each table row.
4. Check that check-in time and the team-name submission deadline are easy to find, then save the schedule card.
