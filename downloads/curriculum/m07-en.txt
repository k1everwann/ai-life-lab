Request for AI

Using these six rows, calculate September and October totals, the increase, and percentage increase to 1 decimal. Show the change in each category. First find categories with an increase greater than 50.

1. Paste the six rows and request into AI to get a spending summary.
2. Use a calculator to check September 2100, October 2350, and an increase of 250.
3. Then send: “Change greater than 50 to at least 50. Which categories appear?” Watch the result change from 1 category to 3.
4. Try: “Add an October meals refund of -100 and recalculate.” October should become 2250. Save the explanation you find clearest.

After trying: compare a possible result

Original six rows: September 2100; October 2350; increase 250; 250÷2100×100≈11.9%. Categories: meals +150, transport +50, services +50. >50 selects meals only, 1 category; >=50 selects 3. Monthly totals do not change. After an October meals refund of -100: October 2250, increase 150, 150÷2100×100≈7.1%; net meals 1250, with all three categories +50.

Check these parts of your result

The original totals are 2100 and 2350: an increase of 250, about 11.9%.
Greater than 50 selects meals; at least 50 selects all three categories.
After the -100 refund, October is 2250: up 150, about 7.1%.

Try a more involved task

Focus on whether a missing amount prevents a complete total.

Fictional category data: September courses 0 TWD, meals 100 TWD; October courses 200 TWD, meals amount missing, books 10 USD. No exchange rate; do not guess amounts.

Answer only: may October’s missing meals amount be treated as zero? Can the known 200 TWD subtotal and separately listed 10 USD be called a complete October total? No zero-baseline percentage calculation is needed.

September known TWD total is 100. October known TWD subtotal is 200 with meals unknown; the separate 10 USD cannot be added directly. Courses increase by 200, but percentage change is undefined with a zero baseline. The complete October total and overall percentage change are unconfirmed; a known subtotal is not a complete total.
